How to interpret the information: Countries may have a road safety coordinating body and a national strategy without evidence that the body produced it. Substantive outputs include policies, strategies, road safety targets, and adopted implementation or action plans when a body’s documented formulation, approval or stewardship role is established. Mere attendance, country-wide plan existence, and meeting records do not establish authorship. “Not confirmed” indicates a gap in available research, not proof that no such institution or document exists.

Scope of this edition: The audit screens 98 original country-plan records and adds Latvia, which was omitted despite its documented plan. Newly credited matches rely on independently located official evidence of the body’s role; other country-level documents remain unlinked where that role cannot be established. A linked institution may have approved, developed, or overseen a strategy without being its sole author. Document links that lead only to an adoption announcement are marked in the document details. The broader historic document catalogue was not supplied as a complete row-level input, so unlinked records should not be read as a comprehensive absence assessment.

Road safety governance and strategy evidence · Country directory. This directory retains the earlier 195-country research overview and expands policy-output attribution where newly verified official evidence supports it. It is a research navigation resource rather than an exhaustive verified world census. The country-level source inventory is broader than the available per-document attribution records; unverified relationships remain explicitly unlinked. Bodies may be historical, proposed or grouped mechanisms; the underlying data should be consulted for unresolved identity and publication questions. The presentation is inspired by public-sector and UNITAR design, without logos or official endorsement.

How to read this research

What do Group 1 and Group 2 mean?

The groups describe the type of coordinating arrangement, not its quality, effectiveness or score. The classification is based on the type of body. Both may have a documented role in shaping a road safety policy, strategy, programme or action plan.

01

Group 1

Government-established coordinating bodies

Official national or subnational councils, commissions, committees and formally designated government coordination or implementation groups. The body has a traceable governmental mandate or appointment to coordinate road safety work. The government may still be the separate authority that formally adopts the strategy.

Examples: Serbia's government coordinating body; Poland's National Road Safety Council; France's Interministerial Road Safety Committee. Tasmania is a subnational case in this group.

24of 27 mechanisms
02

Group 2

Other policy-contributing coordination arrangements

Documented intergovernmental, cross-agency, advisory or parliamentary arrangements that contribute to road safety policy outputs but do not fit the conventional statutory executive road safety council model used for Group 1. Their remit may be narrower than nationwide implementation.

Examples: Canada's Council of Motor Transport Administrators, Sweden's Vision Zero cooperation forum (GNS), and Uganda's parliamentary road safety forum.

3of 27 mechanisms

Classification boundary: Group 1 does not imply direct control over every implementing ministry; Group 2 does not mean an organization is unofficial or ineffective. Membership, policy authority, action ownership and actual delivery are evaluated separately.

Research questions and navigation

1. Who coordinates road safety?

Examine the legal mandate, government hierarchy, formal memberships and functional participants. The global taskforce directory includes 290 records; absence or missing membership is an evidence status, not proof of nonexistence.

2. What did they produce?

Inspect attributed strategies, action plans, implementation programmes and reports. Government adoption, council approval, drafting and agency execution are distinguished.

3. How close to our ideal model?

Browse the detailed 17-domain and 85-cell matrix for 27 mechanisms. Exactly 72 cells define intervention responsibilities; thirteen remain undefined and excluded.

4. What is delivered?

Documented planned responsibilities are not implementation outcomes. Funding, delivery and evaluated road safety impact need separate primary evidence and remain N/A when not verifiable.

Evidence maturity across the focused comparison

Croatia, Cyprus, Estonia, France, Japan, Jordan and Latvia. Original cell codes and 17-domain classifications are preserved from reviewed case-study evidence tables.

Other profiles

Historical aggregate scores for the earlier cases are retained as context. Mexico, Poland, Qatar, Romania and Serbia include updated policy attribution and preliminary examples, with final comparable totals pending.

Not a ranking: More documented roles can indicate stronger governance, more thorough online publication or a more comprehensive audit. Do not interpret evidence coverage as a causal estimate of safety performance.

Country and mechanism universe

26 countries but 27 mechanisms because the United States has two state-level cases. The global directories have a different scope (195 countries and 290 entity records). Do not add the 27 mechanisms to 195 to produce a country count.

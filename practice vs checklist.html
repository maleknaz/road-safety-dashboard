1. How to interpret distance from the ideal

We treat the supplied matrix as an ideal coverage checklist, not as a requirement for 17 separate ministries or 72 compulsory interventions. The unit is the body actually linked to an output, including its documented delivery partners, not the whole country. The US record contains two distinct state bodies, resulting in 27 profiles from 26 countries. Australia is Tasmania; the UK is Northern Ireland. Scope and dates are retained; historical arrangements are not asserted to be current.

Membership dimension. F = formal representation or named leadership in an official roster, founding instrument or documented appointment; P = named partner, working-group function, delivery actor or portfolio interface, not necessarily a formal member; N/A = no link established. Domain coverage = (F + P)/17; observed representation distance = 17 − (F + P). A generic ministry portfolio does not prove a specific duty. F/P assignments are analytical mappings to the workbook, not legal findings. Missing roster entries are not evidence of absence.

Mission dimension. A = matching intervention plus explicit responsible body or coordinating mandate in the reviewed source; P = partial alignment (relevant theme, incomplete match, planned measure or unclear action-level ownership); N/A = unverified; X = unpopulated ideal cell, excluded from the 72-cell denominator. All 85 cells are retained. A can reflect planned responsibility rather than completed delivery. A published strategy alone does not establish assignment of all interventions. Documentary distance = 72 − A, reported alongside P and N/A; it is an upper bound on unconfirmed alignment, not a proven governance deficit. An additional relaxed indicator is 72 − A − P. Actual distance is N/A until a local validation establishes applicable cells and confirmed absences. Do not compare scores as effectiveness or rank countries.

Evidence protocol: exact directory records plus targeted multilingual primary-source searches (official rosters, statutes, government plan descriptions, action annex excerpts). Many full plans and historical rosters were not reviewed cell-by-cell; indexed PDF passages could not be visually rechecked where retrieval failed. Source sets are grouped per profile rather than pinpoint citations for every cell. One analyst coded the evidence; independent validation remains outstanding. Date-mixed rosters and plans are not treated as contemporaneous.

Three types of gaps

Structural: a relevant domain is established as absent from the mechanism and its delivery relationships.

Functional: an applicable intervention is established to lack an accountable owner.

Evidence: the reviewed records do not establish whether the domain or assignment is present or absent.

Current scores measure documentary alignment. Confirmed structural and functional gaps require local validation. The 13 unpopulated ideal cells are excluded from all functional calculations.

What to validate next

Leadership (27), transport (22), police (20), road infrastructure (20) and education (17) are the most visible domains. Public finance and fire/rescue (7 each), environmental protection (6), and civil defence (1) are less visible. These counts support targeted follow-up, not a declaration that these sectors are absent.

Vehicle interventions have no exact matches in this audit. This is a prompt to obtain detailed standards, inspection and fleet-related assignment records, not evidence that the mechanisms lack vehicle safety work.

Read the results together

Estonia and Romania each have 14 documented partner domains but no domains coded F in the reviewed evidence. This means a formal roster was not established by this coding, not that these bodies have no formal members. France, Jordan and Serbia have only one documented domain each and require fuller roster and plan evidence.

5. Lessons learned and implications for the toolkit

1. Organise around functions, not a fixed list of ministries.

Türkiye, Poland and Cyprus illustrate extensive participation and technical groups. Sweden and Canada distribute responsibility through autonomous agencies and jurisdictions. The ideal 17-domain checklist should require a named functional owner or a justified non-applicability decision, not automatically a standing seat for every domain.

2. Establish three levels of authority.

Northern Ireland explicitly separates strategic, tactical and operational fora; Türkiye and Romania link coordination boards to expert groups; Japan anchors central and local planning in legislation. Use an executive chair, permanent secretariat and technical delivery groups with escalation rules.

3. Make the plan an assignment register.

Romania’s amended annex names responsible entities, time frames and financing; Jordan’s adoption statement describes funding/authority/timeline cards for 50 projects and 28 programmes; Brazil’s PNATRANS action dashboard organises products by responsible body. Require each selected matrix cell to have one owner, supporting actors, budget route, milestone, baseline and target.

4. Do not stop at transport and enforcement.

Croatia explicitly plans emergency medical response and first-aid training; Romania assigns emergency and injury-reporting tasks; Chile includes legal, economic and psychological recovery as a post-crash theme. Check rescue, health, rehabilitation, victim support, public finance, environment and civil protection explicitly, especially where policy themes are broad.

5. Design for the jurisdiction.

Tasmania and the US examples are state mechanisms; Northern Ireland is devolved. Canada’s 2025 strategy recommends locally chosen actions without hard national targets, while Uganda’s case is a parliamentary rather than delivery forum. For each cell, specify which tier is legally accountable and how the coordinating body refers or escalates responsibilities beyond its powers.

6. Publish the governance system, not just the strategy.

Publish establishment instrument, dated membership and observer roster, rules of procedure, an action-owner table, funding and periodic progress. Estonia’s annual reporting/budget process, Croatia’s annual financial report requirement and Poland’s permanent secretariat illustrate complementary practices. A gap in publicly available evidence should trigger a request for records, not a punitive score.

7. Assess outcomes separately from alignment.

The 26 country records were selected for linked substantive outputs, not verified effectiveness. This analysis cannot establish whether closer matrix alignment reduces casualties. Test the hypothesis later using consistent outcome series, exposure, legal scope, implementation maturity and confounders; do not equate document availability with performance.

Recommended executive validation process

First validate the institution and dated roster; second crosswalk every eligible cell to a legally responsible actor and supporting institutions; third record a confirmed gap or documented non-applicability only after local review; fourth approve an action plan with dates, budget and indicators; finally review evidence and results annually. Keep the 13 unpopulated cells in a separate research backlog until the ideal is updated.

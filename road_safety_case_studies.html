How to interpret success. We distinguish deliverables (training, plans, road segments or infrastructure assessments) from formal institutional adoption, independently evaluated outcomes, and actual reductions in deaths or serious injuries. A successful workshop or adopted technical guideline is not, by itself, evidence of lives saved.

Names and roles. A person’s appearance in a government publication or evaluation indicates a specific documented relationship, not necessarily that they founded or led the project. Positions are those at the time of publication. The Central Asia programme is counted once but has separate country adoption findings.

Research limits. The dossier includes information reported by official sources, named project partners and independent reports where available. It does not substitute for interviewing implementation leads or acquiring unpublished injury, training, procurement and expenditure data.

Safe and Inclusive Road Design – Central Asia

Not a single original-language title; regional English project and Russian-language technical guidance
Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, UzbekistanRegional / national technical assistance2023-03-13 to 2025-09-0121 publicly named people19 documented institutional links
Initiation and funding
UN Road Safety Fund (funding); UNESCAP (lead implementing UN entity). IRF/iRAP/EASST launched implementation in March 2024.
Implementing organizations
UNESCAP; iRAP (technical lead); EASST; IRF (technical support); local NGOs Road Safety (Kyrgyzstan), Young Generation of Tajikistan, NAFKU (Uzbekistan).
Other collaborating entities
Ministries of Transport; road-design institutes; Kyrgyz traffic police; CAREC Transport Secretariat; ADB; World Bank; EBRD; EIB; UNECE; CAREC Institute.
Funding as documented
USD 500,000 UNRSF; independent evaluation reports 98.57% disbursement.
Field launch and duration
2024-03 (partner field implementation launch)
Status as of Nov 2026
Regional grant completed September 2025; Kyrgyzstan policy adoption active from February 2026 and rollout underway.
Goal and causal logic
Review outdated GOST-SNiP geometric design rules; create inclusive safer road guidance; train engineers; obtain government endorsement; ultimately reduce fatalities and serious injuries.
Implemented activities
Country gap analyses and consultation missions; steering committee; 2024 Tashkent consultations; three technical webinars for 70 practitioners; June 2025 new safer/inclusive design guidance.
Success measures used
Review reports and technical guidance produced; training participation; institutional knowledge uptake; government endorsement of guidance; qualitative effectiveness and sustainability evaluation.
Reported outputs
Three-country design review and inclusive design guidance; approximately 70 technical trainees; 19 evaluation interviews (4 women, 15 men); 17/60 survey responses (28.3%).
Outcome or impact evidence
Kyrgyzstan endorsed the new guidance in February 2026 (confirmed policy adoption); evaluation still found no measured post-adoption crash or fatality reductions.
Monitoring still needed
Ministry standard adoption and enforcement; use of guidance in road design; iRAP safety ratings, training uptake; crash severity and casualties in treated corridors (not yet evaluated).
Evidence-based assessment: Clear documented institutional adoption; no measured casualty effect.
Source confidence: High for dates/partner identities/output; independent evaluation available. Low for actual reduction in road casualties.

Expanding safe school zone models in selected highly urbanized cities in the Philippines

Official project title published in English; locally referred to as Child Road Traffic Injury Prevention (CRTIP) programme.
PhilippinesNational partnership with municipal implementation (Valenzuela and Zamboanga City)2021-09-24 to 2023-10-014 publicly named people17 documented institutional links
Initiation and funding
UNICEF as UN implementing organization, with WHO and UNRSF; scales earlier UNICEF Child Road Traffic Injury Prevention work.
Implementing organizations
UNICEF Philippines; local governments of Valenzuela and Zamboanga; University of the Philippines National Center for Transportation Studies (UP-NCTS); ImagineLaw; Human Development and Empowerment Services; Philippine Legislators Committee on Population and Development.
Other collaborating entities
Department of Transportation; Department of Education; Department of Health; Department of Interior and Local Government; Department of Public Works and Highways; Philippine National Police; Philippine Statistics Authority; Council for the Welfare of Children; WHO; MPTC (private co-funding).
Funding as documented
UNRSF project grant USD 535,364; UNRSF 2022 annual report reports USD 935,364 joint funding with UNICEF. Separate 2021 MPTC multi-year partnership should not be double-counted without financial reconciliation.
Field launch and duration
Earlier child-road-safety programme pilots were operating in these cities in 2019; the specific UNRSF expansion begins 2021.
Status as of Nov 2026
Specific 2021–23 UNRSF project is closed. Longer-term CRTIP policy and school programmes may continue; current site-by-site status not verified.
Goal and causal logic
Improve school routes through child-responsive local design, local implementation teams, education, better injury data, and multi-stakeholder advocacy.
Implemented activities
50 high-risk schools in Valenzuela/Zamboanga assessed and modelled; school route design improvements; Star Rating for Schools assessments; teacher/student road-safety modules and advocacy; youth coalition.
Success measures used
School count modelled; school road safety iRAP SR4S (3+ stars); programme reach; number of students trained; policy or behavioural changes. School injury counts after treatment would be stronger outcome metric but are not published with a comparable counterfactual.
Reported outputs
In 2022, 50 pilot school zones post-assessed; nearly all attained 3+ star ratings; 144 schools supported or targeted in wider rollout; road-safety modules reached 175 schools and 22,265 students; media campaigns reached >570,000 people. 100-school/300,000-beneficiary figures on grant page are goals, not independently confirmed end results.
Outcome or impact evidence
Infrastructure safety proxy improved: post-intervention pilot SR4S 3+ star ratings reported for nearly all schools; four sites listed as 5-star in national planning material. No reliable before-after child fatality or injury effect attributed to this project identified.
Monitoring still needed
Before/after SR4S ratings by individual school; actual count of physical upgrades; school crash injuries and hospital data, design-speed compliance, student commuting exposure, behavioural surveys, expansion beyond two cities.
Evidence-based assessment: Strong intermediate delivery and infrastructure risk rating results; not evidence of a measured reduction in child deaths.
Source confidence: High for partner/period/output counts in UNRSF annual report; weak on causally linked injury effects. Beware unverified 100-school targets.

Country implementation: Two pilot cities: Valenzuela and Zamboanga. Pilot school zone models reported successful in UNRSF communications; exact post-2023 scaling and casualty impacts not independently evaluated here.

Safer Streets for Road Users – Nigeria

Published in English (national official language); local partner uses Safer Roads for Pedestrians and Cyclists in Nigeria.

Support Nigerian Road Safety Strategy II through safe streets for walking and cycling in Abuja; national cycling policy, urban speed management, community-led demonstration and better design practice.
Implemented activities
Abuja pilot protected cycle lane, national cycling policy / strategy development, cycling feasibility corridor study, open-streets/tactical urbanism, engineer/police/community training and regional knowledge exchange.
Success measures used
Kilometres of protected cycling lanes, national policy drafting/approval stage, participants trained, quality of pedestrian/cycling road treatments, speed compliance, cycling uptake. Injury and death outcomes not project-evaluated.

Reduce occupational motorcycle crashes among couriers/fleet employees by integrating protective equipment, risk management, legal compliance, and safe riding standards into employer practices.

Estonian Road Safety Programme 2026–2035 and rolling implementation plans

Liiklusohutusprogramm 2026–2035; elluviimiskava 2026–2029
EstoniaNational (municipal delivery partners)2026-01-01 to 2035-12-314 publicly named people9 documented institutional links
Initiation and funding
Estonian Transport Administration (Transpordiamet) led drafting; Republic of Estonia Government adopted Order No.235 on 18 Dec 2025; Climate Ministry organizes implementation.
Implementing organizations
Government Traffic Commission; Climate Ministry; Transport Administration; Ministry of Interior and its Police and Border Guard Board; Ministry of Education and Research; Ministry of Justice and Digital Affairs; Ministry of Social Affairs; local municipalities and associations.
Other collaborating entities
Police and Border Guard Board; Rescue Board; Consumer Protection and Technical Regulatory Authority; Emergency Response Centre; Labour Inspectorate; Association of Estonian Cities and Municipalities.
Funding as documented
No fixed project total; activities funded through annual government budgets and annually revised 1+3 four-year plans.
Field launch and duration
Government adopted programme 18 Dec 2025; first 2026–2029 rolling plan approved 18 Feb 2026.
Status as of Nov 2026
Adopted national plan and rolling implementation under way; target period continues through 2035.
Goal and causal logic
Safe System national policy: by 2033–2035 three-year average <=22 road deaths and <=272 seriously injured annually; zero deaths under age 16 by 2035; zero pedestrian fatalities in built-up areas by 2035; zero national road deaths in 2050.
Implemented activities
Policy and priority setting across road design, speeds, enforcement, education, emergency response, municipalities; annually updated implementation plan with funded first year plus three forecast years.
Success measures used
3-year average annual fatalities and serious injuries; under-16 deaths; urban pedestrian fatalities; yearly progress reporting for rolling actions.
Reported outputs
Government order Nr 235 adopted 18 Dec 2025; implementation programme 2026–2029 approved 18 Feb 2026; annual updates and accountability via Government Traffic Commission.
Outcome or impact evidence
Targets and implementation architecture established; 2035 results unavailable in 2026. No causal reduction attributable to this new programme yet.
Monitoring still needed
Annual crash death/serious injury series, programme delivery by agency, project investment, speed/seatbelt/VRU behaviour indicators, periodic interim evaluations.
Evidence-based assessment: Clear national policy and monitoring structure; effectiveness on casualty reduction awaits time-series assessment.
Source confidence: High: primary legal order, official programme and explanatory memorandum.

Country implementation: Legally approved, national implementation active. Annual chair report required by 1 July each year.

Ten Step Plan for Safer Road Infrastructure in Tanzania

Official programme title in English; Ministry of Works refers to it as Mpango wa Hatua Kumi
TanzaniaNational infrastructure programme with subnational road networks2020-08-28 to 2023-03-314 publicly named people13 documented institutional links
Initiation and funding
UN Road Safety Fund and World Bank Global Road Safety Facility (with UK Aid); UNECA overseeing UN agency; International Road Federation leading technical delivery.
Implementing organizations
International Road Federation (IRF) lead; iRAP; World Road Association (PIARC); Tanzania Roads Association (TARA); Tanzania Ministry of Works and Transport.
Other collaborating entities
TANROADS; TARURA; Roads Fund Board; National Institute of Transport (NIT); President’s Office Regional Administration and Local Government (PO-RALG); Tanzania professional societies and road safety NGOs.
Funding as documented
USD 480,000 UNRSF-listed project, jointly funded with World Bank/GRSF (UK Aid contribution); do not assume this figure includes all future road works.
Field launch and duration
National first workshop 16–18 March 2021; TanRAP national programme launched September 2022.
Status as of Nov 2026
UNRSF-supported Ten Step pilot completed March 2023; follow-on TanRAP institutional work demonstrably ongoing, including 23-stakeholder development meeting in October 2025.
Goal and causal logic
Build lasting engineering, safer geometric standards, national road assessment and accreditation/skills to support safer built roads and reduce crash severity.
Implemented activities
National steering committee and three workgroups; gap analyses, revisions to design standards, training and accreditations, TanRAP rollout, network assessments and safer-investment specifications.
Success measures used
Engineers and auditors trained/certified; km assessed and iRAP star ratings; road design standard updates; programme/standards adoption; road investments shaped; downstream casualty reduction (not quantified in project report).
Reported outputs
More than 500 local stakeholders trained/certified; over 10,000 km safety-assessed; TanRAP launched Sep 2022; revised geometric design manual and training/accreditation scheme; recommendations for national infrastructure strategy; safety advice applicable to >USD 1bn development bank corridors.
Outcome or impact evidence
Strong delivery of institutional and engineering assessment capacities; specific reductions in crashes and deaths attributable to the pilot not established in public evidence.
Monitoring still needed
Check updated design standards gazette, before/after star rating of treatment corridors, audited km upgraded and capital spend, crash/serious injury rates on improved corridors with exposure/controls.
Evidence-based assessment: Strong intermediate outcomes (capacity, assessments, institutions); sustained TanRAP activity exists; casualty impact still unverified.
Source confidence: High: UNRSF project profile, iRAP and IRF completion, Swahili Ministry of Works report, 2025 iRAP follow-up.

Country implementation: National implementation partners adopted TanRAP institutional mechanism; recommendations and design-standard revisions developed, but official effective legal status of all recommendations requires separate checking.
